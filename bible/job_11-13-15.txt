[13] "if only you would prepare your heart
and lift up your hands to Him in prayer!
[14] get rid of your sins,
and leave all iniquity behind you.
[15] then your face will brighten with innocence.
!!you will be strong and free of fear!!."

---

this verse isn't the encouragement it looks like: you have to know who is speaking these words, and why.
Zophar is Job's friend, sitting across from Him while He's grieving after losing everything, and accusing Him of hidden guilt.
Zophar takes a general truth about repentance and turns it into a weapon, assuming that if Job is suffering, Job must have earned it. that's not how God's love for us works.
these verses are a sobering reminder that how we use Scripture matters just as much as what it says. lifting a verse out of its story can turn a book meant to comfort the brokenhearted into a tool for judging them. !!true wisdom isn't just knowing the right words—it's knowing the person sitting in front of you, and listening carefully before prescribing a formula for their pain!!.
