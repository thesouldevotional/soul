nothing is known about the man behind this book: Malachi means "my messenger" in Hebrew, and many scholars think it may be a title rather than a name. the setting is Jerusalem after the exile, most likely in the 5th century bc. the temple had been rebuilt and sacrifices were offered again, but the enthusiasm of the return was gone: priests offered blind and lame animals, men divorced the wives of their youth, people stopped giving their tithes and wondered aloud whether serving God was worth it.

the book is built as a series of six disputes. God makes a statement, the people object ("how have You loved us?"), and God answers. chapter 1 opens with God's love for Israel and turns on the priests; chapter 2 goes on with the priests and moves to broken marriages; chapter 3 announces the messenger who will prepare the way and the refiner's fire, calls the people back to their tithes and ends with a book of remembrance for those who fear the Lord; chapter 4 closes with the sun of righteousness and the promise of the prophet Elijah before the great day of the Lord.

the last of the minor prophets and the last book of the old testament in christian bibles. the gospels read its messenger and its Elijah as John the Baptist, who prepares the way for Jesus. its promise of a pure offering among all the nations was read by early christian writers, from the Didache on, as a prophecy of the Eucharist.
