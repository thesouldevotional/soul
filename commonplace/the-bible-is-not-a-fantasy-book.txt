the bible is not a fantasy book
↳ [back to index](index.html)
↳ [back to archive](archive.html)
↳ en // [it](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul-it/post.html?p=commonplace/la-bibbia-non-e-un-libro-fantasy.txt)

\ calling the bible a fantasy book runs into two problems.
\ first, the bible isn't one book. it's a library, written over more than a thousand years by dozens of authors, holding completely different genres: law codes, lists of kings with the years they reigned, love poetry, historical and theological narratives, [Ecclesiastes](post.html?p=what-is-it/ecclesiastes.txt) with its philosophical pessimism, real letters with a sender and a recipient. reading all of it as a single genre is like walking into a library, seeing the fiction shelf, and deciding the penal code must be fiction too.
\ second, and more important: Christianity stands on one event, the resurrection, told in the gospels. for most of the twentieth century scholars treated the gospels as something unique — popular writing of an anonymous community, with no parallel in the ancient world. then in 1992 [Richard Burridge published a study at Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burridge_%28priest%29), setting out to prove the gospels were not [greco-roman biographies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel#Genre_and_historical_reliability). he compared them with the ancient biographies that survive, and found he was wrong: they are. today that's the scholarly consensus.
\ and the ancient biography had a firm rule: it was written about a real person, usually recently dead, for readers who could still check whether the facts were true. when an ancient author did drift into legend, he said so — [Plutarch, in his life of Theseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_Lives), warns the reader that from that point on the poets are in charge. the gospels carry no such warning. in its place there are names, places, dates, and witnesses still alive to be questioned.
\ the same goes for the details. in [the case for Christ](https://anthology-lab.github.io/books/the-case-for-christ-lee-strobel.html), Lee Strobel, a journalist who started out as a skeptic, takes the usual objections one by one to the scholars. some of the answers belong here.
\ the oldest material is very early. the creed that opens with [1 Corinthians 15:3-4](post.html?p=bible/1-corinthians_15-3-4.txt) and runs to verse 8 is something Paul says He "received" and "passed on", the technical words rabbis used for handing down a tradition. it's dated 2 to 8 years after the crucifixion: too soon for a legend to grow and take the place of what happened. in 1844 Julius Müller challenged anyone to name a single legend that formed and spread within two generations. according to Strobel, in the next 150 years nobody did.
\ the text we read is the one they wrote. there are more than 5,000 greek manuscripts of the new testament; for Tacitus there's one, copied about 750 years after him. a papyrus fragment of John 18 is dated AD 100-150, a few decades from the original.
\ there are sources outside the bible. Josephus mentions "James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ". Tacitus writes that "Christus" was executed under Pontius Pilate. Pliny the Younger describes christian worship in a letter to the emperor Trajan.
\ and the gospels keep details an inventor would have cut: women as the first witnesses of the empty tomb, at a time when a woman's testimony had no legal weight; the disciples running away; Jesus' anguish in Gethsemane.
\ the book also admits what stays open, like the date of the census of Quirinius in Luke 2:2. but those are a historian's problems. nobody argues about the date of a census in a world that doesn't exist.
\ !!fantasy does the opposite: it builds a world apart from ours, where by definition there's nothing to verify!!.
\ this doesn't prove the resurrection happened. but it shows that "the bible is a fantasy book" has no ground to stand on — it only reveals how little the texts have been read.
